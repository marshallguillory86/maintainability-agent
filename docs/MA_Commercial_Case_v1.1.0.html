A Services Line on maintainability-agent

There is an open-source instrument that measures code health deterministically. It is free, it is public, and it pairs directly with what IBM is already selling. This is the case for Agile Rising building a services offer on top of it.

Marshall Guillory For: Chris Ruch 11 September 2026

The short version

Agile Rising sells AI strategy, operating model design and technical coaching. Every one of those conversations eventually reaches the same question from the client: how do we know the code coming out of this is any good? Today we answer it with judgment. There is no number.

maintainability-agent produces that number. It is a deterministic analyser — same repository in, same score out, every time — calibrated against a reference corpus of 180 repositories across 13 languages. It runs locally in seconds, it states what measured every value, and it refuses to grade when the evidence is too thin to support one. It is not AI and it is not an agent; that is precisely why a build can fail on it.

It is MIT-licensed and public. Agile Rising, its clients, and anyone else may use, modify, integrate and deploy it today. No licence grant, no procurement cycle, no negotiation, no cost.

What is not free is the expertise to read the output, act on it, and build client-specific extensions on top of it. That is the services line, and it is the part only we can sell.

Why this is worth our time specifically

Three reasons, in order of how much money is behind them.

  • It attaches to the IBM relationship rather than competing with it. Bob generates and modernises code. This measures what came out. Bob consumes external tools over MCP, and maintainability-agent ships an MCP server. A Bob client adds it as a tool and audits from inside the IDE — no pipeline change, no new platform, no displacement of anything IBM sells.
  • It gives our AI and operating-model work an outcome metric. We currently sell transformation and report on flow, adoption and practice maturity. None of those tell a CIO whether the estate got healthier. This does, on a scale with a published population behind it, so the before-and-after is defensible rather than anecdotal.
  • The technical-coaching offer becomes evidence-led. Coaching a team on built-in quality is significantly easier when the conversation starts from a measured baseline and a ranked list of what to fix, rather than from an opinion about their codebase.

Four offers, in ascending order of value

All four are consulting engagements. None of them sells software.

01 · EntryCodebase health baseline

A measured starting point

A fixed-scope assessment of a client's repositories producing a scored baseline, an evidence trail showing what was and was not measured, and a prioritised remediation list. Short engagement, low friction, and it is the natural first conversation with any client already worried about AI-generated code volume.

Sells into: existing AI, transformation and coaching accounts · Delivered by: one consultant
02 · RecurringRemediation coaching

Coaching against a measured target

Working with the client's engineering teams to close what the baseline found — test coverage gaps, unenforced practice, modules nothing measures. Every fix is tied to a test that provably fails without it, so improvement is demonstrated rather than asserted. This is the offer that turns a one-off assessment into a retained engagement.

Sells into: any account that took the baseline · Delivered by: coaching staff
03 · StickyPipeline integration

Making the gate real

Wiring the analyser into the client's build so the score becomes a gate rather than a report — thresholds agreed with the client, failures that block, trend reporting to leadership. Determinism is what makes this possible; a judgment that changes between runs cannot fail a build. Once a client's pipeline depends on it, we are in their delivery process.

Sells into: accounts with an existing CI platform · Delivered by: technical coaching
04 · The value leaderHighest margin

Custom analysers and client-specific rules

Every large engineering organisation has standards no off-the-shelf tool checks — internal architectural rules, naming and layering conventions, domain constraints, regulated-industry requirements, legacy patterns specific to their estate. Building analysers and rule sets that encode a client's own standards, and integrating them into their pipeline, is bespoke engineering work at bespoke engineering rates. It is repeatable across clients as a practice while being non-transferable as a deliverable, which is the profile of the best consulting revenue we have.

Sells into: enterprise accounts, IBM i and Z estates, regulated industries · Delivered by: engagement teams

Why this embraces Bob rather than competing with it

This matters for the partner relationship, so it is worth being explicit.

Bob's gates are process gates: approvals, policy enforcement, sensitive-data scanning, red-teaming. They govern how work proceeds. By contrast, maintainability-agent is a product gate: it measures the condition of what came out. A team can have every approval in place and still ship a module nothing tests. Both are needed, and neither substitutes for the other.

IBM's own July announcement quotes survey research finding that 85% of DevSecOps professionals say AI has moved the bottleneck from writing code to reviewing and validating it. Bob accelerates the first half. Selling something that addresses the second half makes us more useful to IBM, not less — and it is a conversation we can have with a Bob client without asking them to buy anything from a third party.

How this is licensed

maintainability-agent is MIT-licensed and published publicly. Agile Rising, its clients, and anyone else may use, modify, integrate and deploy it today. There is no licence to grant, nothing to procure, no negotiation to conduct and no cost to carry. That is the point of choosing that licence — it removes every obstacle between us and selling the services around it.

Custom analysers, rules and integrations built for a specific client under an Agile Rising engagement are Agile Rising work product, owned and billed exactly like any other deliverable we produce. The upstream open-source project is a separate thing that anyone may use, and it stays that way.

That line keeps the commercial position clean: we own what we build for clients, the tool underneath stays free and public, and no client has to take a dependency on a vendor relationship in order to buy the work.
